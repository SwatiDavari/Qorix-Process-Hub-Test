Cybersecurity Management Plan
#############################

Cybersecurity planning translates ISO/SAE 21434 requirements into a concrete, product/project-specific execution framework.

   **Purpose**

   The Cybersecurity Plan defines how cybersecurity activities are planned, coordinated, executed, monitored, and maintained throughout the product lifecycle. It establishes clear accountability, sequencing, and integration of cybersecurity activities to ensure systematic compliance with ISO/SAE 21434 requirements (clauses 6.4.1-6.4.6).

   This process ensures that cybersecurity is treated as a managed engineering discipline rather than an ad-hoc activity, enabling traceability, governance, and auditability across products/projects.

   **Scope and Applicability**

   This process applies to all products/projects involving items or components that are determined to be cybersecurity-relevant. It covers:

   - New development
   - Modification of existing items
   - Reuse in a different operational environment
   - Integration of components developed out-of-context
   - Integration of off-the-shelf components

   If an item is determined to be not cybersecurity-relevant, justification shall be documented and approved, and no further cybersecurity planning activities are required (ISO/SAE 21434 clause 6.4.1).

   **Lifecycle Relevance**

   Cybersecurity planning spans the complete lifecycle, including:

   - Concept and item definition
   - Development and integration
   - Verification and validation
   - Release for post-development

   The plan is established early and evolves iteratively to reflect lifecycle progress, changes, and refinements (clauses 6.4.3, 6.4.6).

   **Preconditions and Dependencies**

   The following inputs typically exist before initiating cybersecurity planning:

   - Product/Project proposal or customer agreement
   - Cybersecurity Interface Agreement (CIA)
   - Organizational CSMS framework
   - Initial product/project plan and milestones

   No formal prerequisite artifacts are mandatory, but alignment with product/project governance is required.

   **Governance and Compliance Context**

   The Cybersecurity Plan is a governed work product subject to:

   - Configuration management
   - Change management
   - Documentation management
   - Requirements management

   It shall be referenced from, or integrated into, the overall product/project plan such that cybersecurity activities remain clearly distinguishable (clause 6.4.4).

   **Tailoring Information**

   Cybersecurity planning may be tailored based on:

   - Product/Project size and complexity
   - Risk exposure and attack surface
   - Degree of reuse or legacy content
   - Organizational role distribution
   - Product/Project-specific execution strategy (For example, if developed as per component-out-of-context CooC approach.)

   Any tailoring shall be justified, documented, and shown to remain sufficient to meet ISO/SAE 21434 objectives (clauses 6.4.5, 6.4.6).

   **Core Concept**

   Cybersecurity planning translates ISO/SAE 21434 requirements into a concrete, product/project-specific execution framework. It ensures that:

   - All required cybersecurity activities are identified
   - Responsibilities are assigned and communicated
   - Dependencies and sequencing are understood
   - Required resources and work products are planned

   This concept directly fulfills ISO/SAE 21434 clause 6.4.2.

   **Cybersecurity Relevance Evaluation**

   Planning begins with evaluating whether the item or component is cybersecurity-relevant. This decision gates the entire CSMS lifecycle.

   TBD-Cybersecurity relevance evaluation overview Image

   If relevance is confirmed, cybersecurity planning becomes mandatory. If not, justification is documented and approved (clause 6.4.1).

   **Activity Identification and Structuring**

   All cybersecurity activities applicable to the product/project are identified, including:

   - Management activities
   - Engineering and analysis activities
   - Verification and validation activities
   - Post-development preparation activities

   Each activity is defined with:

   - Objective
   - Inputs and dependencies
   - Responsible role
   - Required resources
   - Start/end points and duration
   - Expected work products

   This satisfies clause 6.4.2 and clause 6.4.3.

   **Reuse and Out-of-Context Development**

   When reuse or out-of-context development applies, planning explicitly incorporates reuse analysis activities to:

   - Identify modifications and operational environmental changes
   - Analyze cybersecurity impact
   - Assess validity of existing claims and assumptions
   - Identify missing or affected work products

   This ensures continuity of cybersecurity arguments across lifecycle reuse scenarios (clauses 6.4.2, 6.4.3).

   **Interfaces with Other Processes**

   The Cybersecurity Plan interfaces with:

   - Product/Project management
   - Systems engineering
   - Safety management
   - Supplier and interface management

   Distributed activities are aligned through the Cybersecurity Interface Agreement to ensure consistent execution across organizational boundaries (clause 6.4.4).

   **Tailoring Information**

   Conceptual tailoring focuses on:

   - Interface with other process areas as per product/project execution strategy (For example, if developed as per component-out-of-context approach.)
   - Degree of formalization
   - Granularity of activity planning
   - Depth of reuse analysis
