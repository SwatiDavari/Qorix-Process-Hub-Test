   **Purpose**

   Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment (TARA) identifies the cybersecurity assets of an item or component, determines how their compromise could damage road users, identifies the threats and attack paths that could cause that compromise, rates how feasible each attack path is, derives a risk value for every threat scenario, and decides how each risk is treated. TARA is the analytical core of the Cybersecurity Management System (CSMS): every downstream cybersecurity goal, claim, specification, and monitoring trigger traces back to a TARA finding.

   This process implements ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 15 (Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment) and feeds Clause 9.4 (Cybersecurity Goals and Claims) directly.

   **Organizational Scope and Applicability**

   This process applies to every item or component subject to the organization's CSMS, and is invoked:

   - During concept and item definition, once item definition (CS Item / Component Definition) is available.
   - On every change to item / component architecture, functionality, or operational environment.
   - Whenever a new weakness, threat, or vulnerability is identified through Cybersecurity Continual Activities (Perform Vulnerability Analysis) that is not already covered by an existing threat scenario or attack path.
   - Whenever a new or changed cybersecurity requirement is received from an OEM, customer, regulation, supplier, or internal stakeholder.

   After the first baseline, every such trigger enters the change impact analysis (Analyze Change Impact on a Baselined TARA, TARA update triggers). The analysis decides whether the TARA is updated and, if so, which TARA sections are re-analyzed: right from the first section (assets), the change touches through the risk treatment decision, along the TARA traceability chain (see tara_update_rerun_scope and tara_update_examples). A change to a function, interface, or the operational environment is made in the item definition first; the TARA follows the new item definition baseline.

   **Lifecycle Relevance**

   TARA is first performed during concept and item definition and stays under review for the life of the item or component:

   - Initial TARA output (TARA Record) is the direct input to cybersecurity goal and claim derivation (Clause 9.3).
   - Continual Activities (Clause 8) re-uses the asset, damage scenario, and attack path as defined in TARA whenever it analyzes a newly reported weakness (see Perform Vulnerability Analysis), and reports back where that analysis changes an existing threat scenario, attack path, or risk value.
   - A risk treatment decision that is retain or share/transfer feeds cybersecurity claims directly; reduce feeds cybersecurity goals and, from there, cybersecurity specifications and design.

   **Preconditions and Dependencies**

   Typical preconditions include:

   - A draft or baselined item definition (CS Item / Component Definition), including the item's features and interfaces.
   - Product / Project management plan and schedule for the TARA activity.
   - Cybersecurity Plan with TARA scope, roles, and responsibilities defined.
   - Where a baselined TARA is analyzed for change impact: the baselined TARA Record and the triggering input — changed item definition, new or changed requirements (Stakeholder Needs, Consortium / regulatory sources), or vulnerability analysis from Continual Activities.

   **General Workflow**

   Seven activities are performed in sequence, together producing every reviewed part of TARA Record:

   - Identify Assets and Damage Scenarios,
   - Rate Impact,
   - Identify Threat Scenarios,
   - Analyze Attack Paths,
   - Rate Attack Feasibility,
   - Determine Risk Value, and
   - Decide Risk Treatment.

   The reference workbook (Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment Template) groups several of these steps' output fields onto shared worksheets rather than one worksheet per step — the table below shows exactly which template section each step writes into, so the two never need to be reconciled by guesswork:

   Before every baseline, the TARA is technically reviewed and approved by Cybersecurity Product Owner, approved by Cybersecurity Manager, and quality checked by Quality Office in Quality Check of TARA (see the Review Sequence in the workflow description). A baselined TARA is kept current through Analyze Change Impact on a Baselined TARA.

   Step-by-step guidance is provided by Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment Methodology; the impact and attack feasibility rating calculations are described separately in Impact and Attack Feasibility Rating Metrics, which the impact and feasibility steps consume.

   **Governance and Compliance Context**

   This process:

   - Fulfills ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 15 requirements RQ-15-01 through RQ-15-17 and the associated recommendations, and provides the impact- and attack-feasibility-rating basis referenced from Annex F and Annex G of ISO/SAE 21434
   - Provides the risk basis used to justify Cybersecurity Assurance Levels (CAL) assigned to cybersecurity goals under Clause 9.4.
   - Provides evidence expected under UNECE R155 Annex 5 and typical OEM / Tier 1 Cybersecurity Interface Agreement (CIA) clauses for a Tier 2 supplier.
   - Applies quality assurance (ASPICE SUP.1, ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 5.4.4) through the Quality Manager check in Quality Check of TARA.

   **Tailoring Information**

   - Where an item / component is developed out-of-context, analysis is scoped to the item / component and its assumed operational environment as stated in the item definition, rather than a full vehicle system context; traceability to the system-level CSMS execution is maintained through the item definition.
   - Rigor and depth of attack path analysis may be tailored to the criticality and connectivity exposure of the item / component, and to the availability of prior TARA output for reused assets.

   **Tooling Support**

   - TARA workbook. TARA Record is currently maintained as a single structured spreadsheet (one section per work product, one row per record) per Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment Template. Docs-as-code publication of this process area does not require the workbook itself to be reused as tooling, provided the same field structure is kept.
   - Reference and lookup tables. The impact rating and attack feasibility rating scales described in Impact and Attack Feasibility Rating Metrics are maintained as fixed lookup tables alongside the workbook and are not modified per item / component.

.. doc_concept:: Cybersecurity Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment Concept
   :id: doc_concept_qx_cstara_process
   :status: valid
   :version: 1
   :tags: csms_tara

   **Motivation**

   An item or component can be made to behave in ways its road users never consented to if a cybersecurity property of one of its assets is compromised. ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 15 requires that this be analyzed systematically, before the item is built: which assets exist, what happens to road users if each is compromised, how an attacker could realize that compromise, how feasible the attack actually is, and what risk that combination represents. This concept describes how that analysis is structured so every threat scenario stays traceable from asset to risk treatment decision.

   **Core Concepts**

   **Cybersecurity Asset**

   A cybersecurity asset is anything belonging to the item or component whose compromise of a cybersecurity property could lead to damage for a road user: a function, a service, stored or communicated data, or an interface. An asset is identified from the item's features and interfaces (see CS Item / Component Definition) and is confirmed an asset only if at least one of the following properties applies to it:

   - Confidentiality
   - Integrity
   - Availability
   - Authenticity
   - Authorization
   - Non-repudiation

   An element with none of these properties applicable is recorded as considered and excluded, not silently dropped.

   **Damage Scenario**

   A damage scenario describes the adverse consequence to a road user that follows from compromise of one cybersecurity property of one asset. Damage scenarios are identified together with assets, because the two are defined against each other: an asset is confirmed by the existence of at least one plausible damage scenario tied to it.

   **Threat Scenario**

   A threat scenario describes how a threat could exploit a compromised cybersecurity property of an asset to cause a damage scenario. Every threat scenario is uniquely identified and traced to exactly one asset / property / damage scenario combination.

   **Attack Path**

   An attack path is a feasible sequence of actions that an attacker could take to realize a threat scenario. One threat scenario may be realized by more than one attack path; where it is, the attack feasibility rating governing the threat scenario is the least difficult (most feasible) of its attack paths.

   **Risk**

   Risk combines two independently rated quantities for a given threat scenario:

   - The impact rating of its damage scenario (severity of consequence to the road user, across safety, financial, operational, and privacy categories).
   - The attack feasibility rating of the attack path(s) that realize it (difficulty of carrying out the attack).

   The combination is looked up in a fixed risk matrix, never computed ad hoc; see Impact and Attack Feasibility Rating Metrics.

   **Approach**

   TARA is modeled as a seven-step pipeline, each step producing part of the consolidated TARA record (TARA Record). The reference workbook groups several steps' output fields onto shared worksheets rather than giving each step its own — only threat scenario identification (step 3) gets a dedicated section; see the Workflow Step → Template Section table in for the exact, current mapping:

   1. Asset and damage scenario identification (Identify Assets and Damage Scenarios) — assets are derived from the item's features and interfaces, rated against the six cybersecurity properties, and paired with damage scenarios. 2. Impact rating (Rate Impact) — each damage scenario is rated for safety, financial, operational, and privacy impact and combined into one weighted impact value, independent of how the damage scenario could be threatened. 3. Threat scenario identification (Identify Threat Scenarios) — each asset / property / damage scenario combination is analyzed for how it could be threatened. 4. Attack path analysis (Analyze Attack Paths) — feasible attack paths are identified and mapped to the threat scenarios they realize. 5. Attack feasibility rating (Rate Attack Feasibility) — each attack path is rated using the attack-potential-based method, and the governing rating is carried up to its threat scenario(s). 6. Risk value determination (Determine Risk Value) — weighted impact and governing attack feasibility are combined via the risk matrix. 7. Risk treatment decision (Decide Risk Treatment) — avoid, reduce, retain, or share/transfer is decided and recorded per threat scenario, with rationale and a Cybersecurity Assurance Level (CAL) where applicable.

   After the seven steps, the consolidated TARA passes technical review, approval, and quality check before it is baselined (see Review Concept). A baselined TARA is kept current by a change impact analysis (Analyze Change Impact on a Baselined TARA). A change to the item, a new or changed cybersecurity requirement, or an uncovered vulnerability is analyzed to find the first TARA section it touches. A change to a function, interface, or the operational environment is first made in the item definition, which is re-baselined before the TARA is re-analyzed. Re-analysis starts at the first TARA section it touches and continues through risk treatment (asset → damage scenario → threat scenario → attack path → risk value → treatment decision). All other TARA sections keep their content and IDs. The updated record then passes the same review gates before it is re-baselined. The start step per type of change is defined in tara_update_rerun_scope, with worked examples in tara_update_examples.

   **Relationship to Other Processes**

   - Cybersecurity Item Definition (Clause 9.3): supplies the features, interfaces, and operational environment TARA analyzes (CS Item / Component Definition).
   - Cybersecurity Goals and Claims (Clause 9.4): consumes the risk treatment decision — reduce feeds cybersecurity goals, retain / share-transfer feeds cybersecurity claims.
   - Cybersecurity Continual Activities (Clause 8): reuses the asset, damage-scenario, and attack-path basis established here when analyzing a newly reported weakness (Perform Vulnerability Analysis), and reports back where a confirmed vulnerability revises an existing threat scenario or attack path assumption.
   - Cybersecurity Operations and Maintenance (Clause 13): the weighted impact rating method defined here is reused, unchanged, to rate the severity of a field cybersecurity incident.

   **Review Concept**

   The TARA record passes two separate reviews before every baseline:

   - Technical review (Cybersecurity Product Owner): Is the analysis correct, complete, and traceable? Uses Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment Review Checklist.
   - Quality check (Quality Office): Was the process followed? Uses TARA Quality Check Checklist. No re-review of technical content.

   Approval is given by Cybersecurity Product Owner and the project-allotted Cybersecurity Manager. Both review results, the approvals, and — for an update — the change impact analysis are kept in TARA Review Record. The Quality Manager is reused from the Quality House; no separate cybersecurity quality role exists.

   **Attack-Potential-Based Attack Feasibility (Annex G.2)**

   Attack feasibility in TARA is rated with the attack-potential-based method of ISO/SAE 21434 Annex G.2, distinct from the CVSS-based approach ISO/SAE 21434 Annex G.3 used by Cybersecurity Continual Activities for a vulnerability discovered after release (see CVSS-Based Vulnerability Analysis and Management). Both approaches feed the same risk value and risk treatment methods (Clause 15.8, 15.9); See Impact and Attack Feasibility Rating Metrics for the full method.

   **ASPICE Alignment**

   This process supports:

   - MAN.7 outcomes, by providing the risk basis project risk management draws on for cybersecurity risk.
   - SEC.1-class outcomes in cybersecurity-tailored ASPICE assessment models, by providing traceable threat scenario, attack path, and risk records.
   - SUP.1 outcomes, by an independent quality check of the TARA record before each baseline.

   **Tailoring Information**

   - Where an item / component is developed out-of-context, analysis is scoped to the item / component and its assumed operational environment as stated in the item definition, rather than a full vehicle system context.
   - Depth of attack path analysis may be tailored to the criticality and connectivity exposure of the asset under analysis, and to whether a comparable attack path already exists in TARA Record for a reused asset.
