   **Purpose**

   The purpose of the Cybersecurity Item / Component Definition process is to establish a clear, consistent, and cybersecurity-relevant definition of the item or component that forms the foundation for subsequent cybersecurity activities.

   This process ensures that:

   - The item boundary is clearly defined
   - Item functions and preliminary architecture are understood
   - The operational environment is described with cybersecurity relevance
   - Inputs to Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment (Clause 15) are complete and consistent

   This process directly supports compliance with ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 9.3 and provides critical inputs to TARA (Clause 15) and cybersecurity concept (Clause 9.5). It also supports UNECE R155 expectations for risk identification (which also corresponds to ASPICE - MAN.7).

   **Organizational Scope and Applicability**

   This process applies to:

   - All cybersecurity-relevant items or components developed or reused
   - New developments, derivatives, and reused systems
   - Items developed in isolation using assumed or generic contexts

   It is applicable across vehicle-level, system-level, and component-level developments.

   **Lifecycle Relevance**

   The item definition is an early lifecycle activity and serves as a prerequisite for:

   - Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment (ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 15)
   - Cybersecurity goal definition (Clause 9.4)
   - Cybersecurity concept (Clause 9.5)
   - System and software cybersecurity requirements derivation (Clause 10)

   The definition remains a living artifact and may be refined as the lifecycle progresses.

   **Preconditions and Dependencies**

   Typical preconditions include:

   - Availability of high-level vehicle or system architecture information (if applicable / relevant)
   - Availability of requirements from consortium, or regulatory sources (if applicable / relevant)
   - Stakeholder / customer requirements (if applicable / relevant)
   - Documentation from earlier or reused developments (if applicable / relevant)
   - Known operational context assumptions
   - Target delivery market(s) / regions, or an assumed market set where the item is developed out-of-context, with the resulting regulatory applicability and market-specific features (if applicable / relevant)

   **Governance and Compliance Context**

   This process:

   - Fulfills ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 9.3 requirements
   - Provides mandatory inputs to Clause 15 (TARA)
   - Supports UNECE R155 expectations for risk identification
   - Aligns with ASPICE for Cybersecurity 2.0 MAN.7 and SEC.1 process outcomes
   - Applies quality assurance (ASPICE SUP.1, ISO/SAE 21434 Clause 5.4.4) through the Quality Manager check in Quality Check of Cybersecurity Item / Component Definition

   **Tailoring Information**

   The depth and formality of the item definition may be tailored based on:

   - Item criticality and exposure
   - Development context (vehicle, ECU, middleware, software component)
   - Reuse vs. new development
   - Regulatory and customer expectations

   **Core Concept**

   The item definition establishes an understanding of what is being protected from a cybersecurity perspective. It describes:

   - What constitutes an item / component?
   - How does the item / component behave?
   - Where does the item / component operate?
   - How does the item interact with its environment?

   This concept ensures traceability between technical design, risk analysis, and cybersecurity decisions.

   **Practical Interpretation**

   The item definition should be created collaboratively across system, software, and cybersecurity disciplines. Key practices include:

   - Using architecture views already familiar to engineering teams
   - Avoiding premature design detail
   - Focusing on cybersecurity-relevant aspects only

   **Best Practices**

   - Explicitly document assumptions and constraints
   - Capture interfaces comprehensively
   - Reuse existing architectural artifacts where possible
   - Maintain consistency with safety and system engineering views

   **TARA Enablement**

   A high-quality item definition:

   - Reduces ambiguity in threat identification
   - Improves consistency of risk ratings
   - Minimizes late discovery of attack paths

   **Review Concept**

   The item definition passes two separate reviews before baseline:

   - Technical review (Cybersecurity Product Owner): Is the content correct and sufficient for TARA? Uses Item Definition Review Checklist.
   - Quality check (Quality Office): Was the process followed? Uses Item Definition Quality Check Checklist. No re-review of technical content.

   Approval is given by Cybersecurity Product Owner and the project-allotted Cybersecurity Manager. Both results and approvals are kept in CS Item / Component Definition Review Record. The Quality Manager is reused from the Quality House; no separate cybersecurity quality role exists.

   **ASPICE Alignment**

   This process supports:

   - MAN.7 outcomes by enabling risk analysis inputs
   - SEC.1 outcomes by establishing the basis for cybersecurity goals and requirements
   - SUP.1 outcomes by an independent quality check of the work product before baseline

   The item definition bridges organizational CSMS intent with product/project-level execution.

   **Tailoring Information**

   Concept depth may vary depending on whether:

   - The item / component is developed out of context
   - The operational environment is fixed or assumed
   - The item / component is reused with known history
